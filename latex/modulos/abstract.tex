\begin{abstract}
\noindent
In corporate analytical environments, light and heavy queries often share the same infrastructure, and a single heavy query can degrade service for every user. To avoid this interference, organizations either oversize their clusters or separate them through gateways with static rules, based on who submits a query rather than on how much it demands. This work presents DyraSQL, a mechanism that decides, before execution and without modifying the SQL, which Trino cluster (small, medium, or large) each query should be sent to. The decision combines into a Score the data volume estimated by EXPLAIN (TYPE IO) over Apache Iceberg tables, the structural complexity of the query, and its execution history, with a decision cache in PostgreSQL. The prototype, integrated with Trino Gateway, was evaluated in a local environment with 18 queries executed on all three clusters. The initial formulation selected the smallest adequate cluster in 3 of 15 cases and undersized 2, because the volume read does not capture memory-intensive operators. By including these operators and calibrating the parameters through grid search, DyraSQL selected the correct cluster in 14 of 15 queries without undersizing (13 of 15 under cross-validation), with median decision times of 181 ms without cache and 7 ms with cache. The results indicate that DyraSQL can act as a demand-driven load balancer, automatically isolating heavy queries from light ones.
\\\textbf{\keyword{Keywords: }} Query routing. Workload isolation. Trino. Apache Iceberg. Data lake. Trino Gateway.
\end{abstract}
