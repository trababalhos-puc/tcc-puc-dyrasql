\begin{abstract}
\noindent
In corporate analytical environments, light and heavy queries often share the same infrastructure, and a single heavy query can degrade service for every user. To avoid this interference, organizations either oversize their clusters or separate them through gateways with static rules, based on who submits a query rather than on how much it demands. This work presents DyraSQL, a mechanism that decides, before execution and without modifying the SQL, which Trino cluster (small, medium, or large) each query should be sent to. The decision combines into a Score the data volume estimated by EXPLAIN (TYPE IO) over Apache Iceberg tables, the structural complexity of the query, and its execution history, with a decision cache in PostgreSQL. The prototype, integrated with Trino Gateway, was evaluated with a synthetic set of 18 queries executed on three local clusters, 15 of which have at least one viable cluster. Among them, the initial formulation selected the smallest adequate cluster in 3 cases and undersized 2, because the volume read does not capture memory-intensive operators. With these operators and parameters calibrated through grid search, DyraSQL selected the correct cluster in 14 of the 15 on the calibration set, without undersizing, and in 13 under cross-validation, with median decision times of 83 ms without cache and 5 ms with cache. The results are preliminary evidence of prior classification; isolation under concurrency was not evaluated.
\\\textbf{\keyword{Keywords: }} Query routing. Workload isolation. Trino. Apache Iceberg. Data lake. Trino Gateway.
\end{abstract}
